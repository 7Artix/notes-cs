# Stage 1: Self-Repairing Voxel Organisms

The first stage is an iterative attempt at **NCA (Neural Cellular Automata)** and **CPPN/HyperNEAT**. In this stage, we will not create artificial life in the truest sense, but rather **practice the transition process from genotype to phenotype**.

## Background

In the **NCA** approach, the **world size is fixed**, and there is essentially **no distinction between the organism and the environment** (every grid cell is the organism). These characteristics make NCA seem less like a biological entity and more like a **game played on a chessboard**.

In the **CPPN/HyperNEAT** approach, the **structure of the final phenotypic network is artificially prescribed**, which severely limits the structure's own evolutionary capacity.

This proposal aims to solve these problems by making **the environment fundamentally distinct from the organism**, with **no limits on the size of the environment**. The environment simply serves as the space where the organism resides.

## Expected Features

First, a **target 3D structure** will be designed and represented using **voxels**, allowing it to be composed of **multiple materials**.

A **zygote** is placed in the environment, and the iteration begins:
- The voxel organism gradually **develops** into the pre-designed 3D structure.
- When a part of the voxel organism is **amputated**, it can **naturally regenerate**.
- By inspecting the voxel organism, its **internal structure** and material distribution are identical to the pre-designed composition.

As the voxel organism develops, it gradually grows from a single zygote into the expected 3D object with an internal structure.

## Proposed Implementation

We will use the **Genotype + Phenotype** model.

The cell itself possesses a **cell vector**, encompassing dimensions such as:
- **Cell type.**
- **Cell viability (survival status).**
- **Hidden dimensions.**

Similar to NCA, the **hidden dimensions** are used to facilitate the emergence of certain cellular characteristics, influence surrounding cells, and serve as a reference when new cells are generated.

Unlike NCA, this project plans to use **CPPN** as the gene to determine the phenotype generation pattern.

### Genotype

A CPPN network is used as the gene, acting as the state transition function $F$ that controls cell updates, division, and apoptosis:
- **Inputs:**
	- The cell's own state vector $\boldsymbol{v}_i^{(t)}$.
	- The aggregated feature vector $\boldsymbol{E}_i^{(t)}$ of the surrounding 26-neighborhood environment and cells.
- **Outputs:**
	- The increment of the cell state vector $\Delta \boldsymbol{v}_i$.
	- The increment of the aggregated feature vector $\Delta \boldsymbol{E}_i$.
	- A selection of 0 or 1 position $p_i$ from the surrounding 26 grid spaces, deciding whether to generate a new cell $\boldsymbol{w}_i$.

$$
(\Delta \boldsymbol{v}_i, \Delta \boldsymbol{E}_i, p_i, \boldsymbol{w}_i) = F(\boldsymbol{v}_i^{(t)}, \boldsymbol{E}_i^{(t)})
$$

### Phenotype

During each step, the system **traverses all cells** and runs the **CPPN** process once for all living cells. This handles the **generation** of new cells and **updates** their own cell vectors/environmental vectors. If a cell has died, it is **removed**.

## Training Method

The object of training is the **CPPN** acting as the gene, utilizing the **NEAT + GA** algorithms for dynamic mutation.

A **Genetic Algorithm (GA)** is used for **fitness evaluation** and **elimination** of the generated individuals.

At appropriate times, **gene crossover** can be introduced, hybridizing the genes of individuals with high fitness according to the logic of the NEAT algorithm.

Because this scheme does not include the concept of energy, the organism might continuously produce new cells in the early stages without maintaining a stable state. To address this, a virtual **volume limit** can be set (slightly larger than the target voxel model). Once the organism reaches a certain degree of growth, it enters the elimination and selection step, accelerating the convergence process.

## Future Directions

In subsequent developments, an **energy system** can be designed. An energy storage dimension can be added to the cell vector, and an initial energy value will be granted to the zygote. Energy can be **transmitted** between cells, and during cell division, energy is simultaneously **redistributed**.

With the introduction of the energy mechanism, cells can no longer divide infinitely; they stop when **energy is depleted**. The fitness evaluation can then shift from direct structural comparison to an **energy reward** format. When a structure matching the target is evolved, the cells are given more energy rewards, and fitness is determined by assessing the overall survival time.

The energy system also makes it easier to further observe the **self-repair process** of voxel organisms. When energy is exhausted, further repair will lack the necessary energy support.

Additionally, environmental systems such as elimination, reproduction, and crossover can all be redesigned based on the energy system to happen automatically.

# Stage 2: Artificial Life with Only Neural Networks

## Inspirations from Real Biology

**The birth of nerves stems from coincidence.** The prototype of **immune cells** laid the foundation for the emergence of nerve cells. The onset and end of the **Snowball Earth period** allowed **calcium ions** to enter the primitive organisms' environment through geological activity, becoming a crucial factor in cell survival. Furthermore, the emergence of **calcium pumps and calcium channels** provided the conditions for the transmission of **nerve impulses**. To improve the **signal-to-noise ratio** of signal transmission, organisms evolved an upgraded solution using sodium and potassium ions as mediums.

The nervous system is not biologically special; it is simply a subset of cells that diverged functionally under natural selection. Therefore, **the nervous system is not an independent entity**. However, because the virtual environment lacks **interactive interfaces** with virtual life, most cellular functions are difficult to implement and verify. The nervous system, on the other hand, can allow observers to tangibly experience its power through inputs and outputs.

Compared to biological systems, traditional **artificial neural networks** are more like **tools**. Their structures are fixed and **lack dynamic variations and possibilities**. Furthermore, they are purely mathematical models where the topology **only represents logical connections**, and the weights on the edges are merely numerical parameters **rather than actual physical structures**.

A real **nervous system** is a **spatial network** with actual **scale** and **dimensions**. Biological organisms develop from a single zygote, and this dynamic developmental process and exquisite framework cannot be matched by artificially designed hierarchical structures.

The nervous system contains **loop topologies** internally and is a system that **operates continuously over time**. Based on such structures, features like **short-term memory**, **habituation**, and **adaptability** emerge. Even if external stimuli disappear, **the circuits can maintain self-excitation**, allowing the organism to **"mind-wander."** RNNs, which utilize this concept, possess preliminary short-term memory capabilities.

Nerve cells in nature have certain **operating parameters** that serve as references. In the design plan, we should avoid directly hardcoding these parameters, but we can design **mechanisms and logic**, and observe whether the **emergent attributes** align with expectations after training. These mechanisms and logic originate from the physical world. Since the computer world lacks the rigorous environmental constraints of reality, these constraints must be preserved as a baseline:
- Accumulated input nerve impulses (membrane potential)
- Trigger threshold for output nerve impulses
- Refractory period (vesicle depletion)
- Ion concentration recovery rate
- Natural decay rate of membrane potential

## Expected Features

In the early stages, artificial life evolved using genetic algorithms usually does not have an advantage over traditional artificial neural networks in functional applications, because highly efficient gradient descent algorithms cannot be used for rapid iteration, resulting in slow convergence. However, the very existence of artificial life holds intrinsic value. We can observe the **evolutionary process**, the **emergence of various structural features**, the **generation of spontaneity**, and the **development of short-term memory**.

It is expected that the **artificial life with only neural networks** implemented in this stage should possess **problem-solving capabilities similar to small-parameter traditional artificial neural networks**, such as implementing logic gates, simple classification, simple motion control, or even image recognition. Furthermore, it should **show advantages** in dimensions like **spontaneity**, **short-term memory**, and **adaptability**, and exhibit **emergent features** consistent with biological reality.

## Proposed Implementation

### Topological Representation and Data Structures of Life

Beyond neural networks, the most crucial part of simulating life via computers is representing its topology and using suitable data structures for storage and efficient computation. Various graphs or voxels are usually used to store life, but existing solutions generally have limitations.

#### Limitations of Graph Approaches
Current **mainstream artificial neural networks use directed graphs for their topology**, which usually only describe the logical connections between nodes without representing actual physical dimensions in space. This optimizes performance and computational efficiency for purely logical operations, but for an embodied organism, it **lacks volume**, **fails to form effective spatial features**, and **restricts the formats of interaction and input/output signals**.

#### Limitations of Voxel Approaches
The **core feature of voxel models is the emphasis on structure**, but using voxels heavily **restricts the flexibility and degrees of freedom in gene expression**. Like terrain in Minecraft, all structural features are confined to a grid and cannot expand freely as needed. This is a fatal flaw when attempting to express cell division generating new cells.

Biological organisms continuously divide from a zygote. During this process, **cells squeeze against each other and expand as a whole**. Voxel-based schemes are fundamentally unable to handle these squeezing and expanding requirements.

Furthermore, real **cells come in various morphologies**, and their connection methods differ greatly. When representing non-standard cells with long spans, such as neurons, the voxel approach becomes overly cumbersome.

#### Graph Topology with Dimensional Structures
To represent life structures more broadly and accurately, rather than confining them to abstracted artificial neural networks, we need to design a **specialized graph** that can represent specific **dimensions and connection structures**.

**Cells are treated as nodes in the graph, and physical connections between cells are represented by directed edges. The edges have parameter lists to record connection distances and other attributes between nodes.** Through this graph topology, artificial life can be stored in a flexible, scalable format with real physical structures, fulfilling various needs:
- **Structurally Meaningful I/O Interfaces:** Connections with physical structures and specific dimensions can delineate the **structural boundaries** of the artificial life, facilitating targeted **inputs and outputs** from the simulated environment.
- **Feature Emergence:** During evolution, the **emergence of corresponding biological features** can be observed through specific structures.
- **Dynamic Adjustment:** During **cell division**, **new nodes can be dynamically inserted** into existing connections. By designing edge weights, different parts of the graph can experience **tensile forces**, thereby triggering further **division or apoptosis** actions at corresponding cellular locations.
- **Scalability:** The physical structure provides a prerequisite foundation for the subsequent introduction of an **energy system**.

Information transfer between cells is both **unidirectional** (e.g., neural synapses) and **bidirectional** (e.g., chemical or electrical signals propagating through gap junctions). Moreover, **connections between cells are usually local**; any single cell only connects to a finite number of other cells. Given these features, storing the organism using **sparse matrices** is **undesirable**; the massive amount of data would be disastrous for both storage and computation.

In engineering implementation, we must avoid recursive deadlocks caused by nested nodes. Therefore, we plan to adopt a decoupled architecture utilizing a **Node Dictionary** and a **Global Sparse Adjacency List**:
- **Node Dictionary:** $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}} = \{\boldsymbol{v}_1, \boldsymbol{v}_2, ..., \boldsymbol{v}_N\}$, stores cell attributes for global retrieval.
- **Sparse Adjacency List:** $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}} = \{\boldsymbol{e}_{ij}\}$, records unidirectional connection relationships, distances, and other parameters.

In summary, in this design plan, **the actual format of the artificial life is a graph capable of representing physical structures, stored using a dictionary and an adjacency list.**

### Genotype

The gene is implemented using a **CPPN** network:
- **Inputs:**
	- Connection relationships and parameters of the corresponding cell in the adjacency list.
	- The cell's own state vector.
	- The cell's surrounding environmental vector.
- **Outputs:**
	- The increment of the cell's own state vector.
	- The increment of the cell's surrounding environmental vector.
	- State updates for the synapse list. If a new connection appears, it is updated to the adjacency list.
	- (Potential) new cell generation. Corresponding objects and connection parameters are added to the adjacency list, resulting in a new branch in the graph.

The gene behavior design describes several anticipated behavioral patterns of artificial life:
- **Nerve Bundling (Fasciculation) and Contact Guidance:** Newly formed nerve cells will retrieve targets based on the adjacency list and make connections. Thus, synapses will always search along existing pathways, realizing nerve fasciculation. The example of the **recurrent laryngeal nerve** illustrates this behavior well: the control nerve from the brain to the larynx goes down into the chest cavity before returning upwards. Structures that are physically close are far apart path-wise.
- **Natural Formation of Loop Structures:** Because cell connections always **search along reverse pathways**, loop structures are formed naturally. In real biological nervous systems, **loop structures account for a very high proportion**.
- **Pheromone System Controlling Connection Establishment:** Through the **cell's surrounding environmental vector**, a dimension of information is reserved to represent the influence the cell exerts on its internal environment. This can be used to determine changes in cell states, the establishment of connections, and the formation of new structures.
- **Diversity in Gene Expression:** Because **the generation of branches** and **the establishment of synapses** are not fixed, and they work jointly with **input signals**, the genotypic to phenotypic expression truly achieves diverse and stochastic characteristics.

### Phenotype

**Phenotypic construction and neural signal transmission triggers occur simultaneously**, progressing **dynamically over time**. Information transfer features a **time accumulation effect**.

In the initial state, all preset **input nodes** and **output nodes** are uniformly connected to the **zygote**.

Once evolution begins, the system **traverses** all cell nodes at a **fixed clock cycle frequency**. Based on the **signal inputs from upstream nodes and the current internal state** from the previous time cycle, it updates the **signal output** for the current cycle. Additionally, structural growth is processed for each cell, including the **generation of new nodes**, **synapse establishment**, **state and environment updates**, and **apoptosis**.

Due to the presence of **input signals** and internal **circuit self-excitation** generated by loop structures, **the same genotype will map to different phenotypes**—namely, specialized gene expressions driven by environmental influences.

Although structurally engineering solutions and answers goes against our core design philosophy, the simulation program lacks the natural constraints of the real world. Therefore, corresponding **limiting logic** must be established to await the **emergence of corresponding concepts** during evolution.

For instance, regarding neural signal transmission, logic should be designed to simulate physical **transmission constraints**, including the **natural decay rate of membrane potential**, the **recovery rate of vesicles**, and the **trigger threshold for signal transmission**.

To translate the implicit constraints of the physical environment into computer-executable mechanisms, two core hidden state variables are defined: the **accumulated membrane potential** $U_i^{(t)}$ and the **available ion concentration/vesicle reserve** $R_i^{(t)}$. At time $t$, node state updates and signal transmission follow these predefined rules:

**Membrane Potential Accumulation and Natural Decay:**

$$
U_i^{(t)} = U_i^{(t-1)}\cdot(1-\lambda) + \sum_{j\in \mathcal{N}(i)}S_j^{t-\Delta t_{ji}} \cdot w_{ji}
$$

- $\lambda$: Natural decay rate of the membrane potential.
- $\Delta t_{ji}$: Physical transmission delay calculated via the topological distance of nodes.
- $w_{ji}$: Connection attributes, such as **excitatory** and **inhibitory connections**.

**Vesicle and Ion Concentration Consumption and Recovery:**

$$
R_i^{(t)} = \min\left[ R_{\text{max}}, (R_i^{(t-1)}-\alpha_{\text{cost}}\cdot S_i^{(t-1)}+\beta_{\text{recover}}) \right]
$$

- $\alpha_{\text{cost}}$: Consumption per neural impulse.
- $\beta_{\text{recover}}$: Recovery amount of vesicles or ion concentration.

**Trigger Threshold and Nerve Impulse:**

$$
S_i^{(t)} = \left\{
\begin{array}{ll}
1, & U_i^{(t)} \ge \theta_{\text{th}} \text{ and } R_i^{(t)} \ge \beta \\  
0, & \text{otherwise}  
\end{array}
\right.
$$

- $\theta_{\text{th}}$: Trigger threshold for outputting a nerve impulse.

## Training Method

The target of training is the **CPPN** network acting as the gene. Due to the involvement of complex logical rules and the fact that the process from genotype to phenotype is influenced by inputs, gradient chains will shatter, rendering backpropagation and gradient descent iterative methods ineffective. Therefore, **Genetic Algorithms (GA)** should be used for evolutionary screening and iteration.

Individuals are evaluated for fitness against target functions, and better-performing genotypes are selected for iteration. Simultaneously, the scale of both genotype and phenotype is monitored, and a **penalty mechanism** is introduced to control the **scale of the network structure** and **prevent uncontrolled expansion**.

Once functions are realized, further **structural simplification screening** can be conducted to select simpler genes among those exhibiting similar performance.

Furthermore, artificial life possessing **multiple capabilities** can be trained to verify its **gene information density**. As a benchmark, we can **independently train** different organisms to satisfy individual requirements, calculate the **sum of their genetic information**, and **compare** it against the genetic information of an organism possessing multiple capabilities simultaneously.

The significance of artificial life with a physical structure is strongly bound to the existence of an **environment**. Only artificial life situated in an environment finds value in possessing a structure. Through **interaction between the structure and the environment**, including **energy** and **physical conditions**, the artificial life can develop more comprehensive abilities. Moreover, the **cell's local environmental information vector** can be abstracted to share a global external environmental information vector.

The relationship between the environment and the organism can be designed as: high-dimensional beings living in a low-dimensional space map the environmental information at the organism's location onto the organism's true structural network. Through input/output interfaces, the environment can interact with the artificial life.

Multiple life forms can co-evolve simultaneously in the environment. When a cell node completely **disconnects** from all other cells, if it hasn't died, it is judged as a **new individual**, **undergoes mutation**, and begins its own developmental process.

# <span style="color: #ffd700">★</span> Stage 3: Embodied Artificial Life

## Background

The ultimate goal of designing artificial life is to enable it to survive in the **real world** just like biological life, mastering the survival rules of the real world and playing an important role within it.

The current mainstream paradigm for robot training is more like **building the shell first**, then independently evolving the **brainstem and spinal cord**, and finally forcing a **brain** on top (or omitting it entirely). This contradicts the evolutionary process of biological functions. If the ultimate goal is an artificial organism possessing **both intelligence and a physical body**, then **somatic control, perception, and intelligence must be achieved synchronously and co-evolve**, because the structure itself also bears computational capacity—namely, **morphological computation**.

Furthermore, neural networks can only output data formats strictly identical to their training phase during operation, which limits the model's **generalizability** and **multimodal interaction capabilities**. A true embodied organism should support multimodal inputs at the architectural design level and generate spontaneous responses during genuine physical and environmental interactions.

## Expected Features

While the physical **structure** of artificial life evolved in a **simulated environment is unconstrained**, in reality, it is **limited by current engineering technologies**, making it **impossible to physically replicate arbitrary structures**. Particularly in the critical dimensions of **sensors**, **actuators**, **energy supply**, **information transmission**, and **material properties**, the embodiment of artificial life must adapt to existing engineering capabilities to successfully manufacture actual physical organisms.

Despite objective constraints, enabling **physical structures** to **co-evolve** with the **nervous system** remains a worthy goal. Only when the physical body and the internal nervous system are generated through co-evolution can the two systems cooperate optimally. This is also the core significance of **morphological computation**: the **physical structure of the body itself** bears part of the burden for **computation and environmental adaptation**.

This stage plans to implement a **hardware-embodied artificial life** framework that gradually transitions from a **simulated environment** to a **real physical structure**, featuring the following characteristics:
- **Co-evolution of Body and Intelligence:** The final emergent entity might be a "robotic kitten" or a "robotic snake"; its morphology depends on the optimal solution it evolves to adapt to its multimodal environment.
- **Distributed Architecture:** Abandoning a centralized single-compute brain, it adopts a hierarchical structure akin to a biological torso, spinal cord, and brain. This grants each physical module local neural processing capabilities, such as reflexes and preprocessing, while necessary or complex information is still funneled to the brain for centralized decision-making.
- **Separation of Genotype and Phenotype:** The gene not only determines the assembly morphology of the body but also dictates the **local neuron connection patterns** and **synaptic plasticity rules** when the system operates in the real world.
- **Multimodal Perception and Interaction:** Breaking free from traditional single-task limitations, the embodied organism will spontaneously develop an understanding of and reactions to human body language, sounds, and environmental stimuli in complex virtual/real multimodal environments. For instance, seeing a target object prompts **approach**, seeing body commands prompts **obedience**, hearing sounds prompts **responses**, feeling attacks prompts **evasion**, and receiving code commands prompts **execution**.

## Proposed Implementation

This approach requires establishing a standardized **parameterized hardware module library** and a set of **graph network generation rules that support the simultaneous generation of morphology and nervous systems**.

### Hardware Module Library and Distributed Topology

The body of the artificial life is discretized into a series of **standardized, parameterized modules** possessing realistic dynamic parameters and manufacturability.
- **Joint Modules:** E.g., joint modules with rotational degrees of freedom (corresponding to the upper arm, forearm, etc.), containing internal actuators like motors and reducers, with real physical parameters (mass, torque). They feature parameterized interfaces to adjust features like length. Additionally, they are equipped with input interfaces like stress/tactile sensors and temperature/humidity sensors.
- **Distributed Neural Centers (MCU Units):** An MCU is embedded inside every module. Each module contains a local neural network to process local reflexes and aggregate sensor data. For complex perception and decision-making, "brain" and "spinal cord" modules are introduced to process central information and generate behavior.
- **Perception Modules:** E.g., vision modules, hearing modules, which can be located anywhere on the body, but their morphological generation pattern will undergo adaptive selection, ultimately evolving into a rational layout.
- **System Bus:** All modules are topologically connected via a physical bus, simulating biological nerve pathways, providing routing for inter-module communication, central perception, and control.

### Genotype

The genotype at this stage is a **CPPN with cross-scale expression capabilities**, functioning simultaneously as a **morphogenesis generator** and a **network builder**.

Because the evolutionary direction includes both the **morphological structure** and the internal **neural network structure**, designing isolated construction genes would lead to the **credit assignment problem**. Therefore, we introduce a construction mechanism with cross-scale expression capability as the genotype, ensuring the growth of the body and the neural network is synchronous.
- **Intra-module Network:** Evolution begins with a single neuron (zygote) inside a module. The CPPN determines the generation and connection patterns of neurons based on the current node state and environment. The module's own sensors and actuators are also directly exposed as nodes for the network to connect to.
- **Morphogenesis:** When boundary conditions are met, the CPPN instantiates a bodily module based on the state and extends the network into the newly generated module.

### Phenotype

The phenotype is divided into two stages: the **Virtual Evolution Stage** and the **Physical Embodiment Stage**.

#### Virtual Evolution
In the simulated physics engine, the initial node begins **intra-module network construction** and **morphogenesis** based on the genotype. It is worth noting that the generative logic running inside all modules is completely identical, just as the same genes exist in every cell.
The configurations composed of modules are tested in a multimodal virtual environment. At this point, the genes still exert influence, controlling changes in the phenotype under environmental influence to establish mechanisms that adapt to the environment.
**Morphological architecture needs to be stable.** During virtual evolution, individuals that cannot stably maintain their morphological traits are **culled** via screening mechanisms, making physical embodiment feasible.

#### Physical Embodiment
Individuals with high fitness are selected from the virtual environment, and their morphological architecture is **assembled into a physical entity** using real hardware modules. Within the real structure, the **gene expression starting from the initial node restarts in the real-world environment**.
Initially, although the assembled body has physical connections, it is completely paralyzed logically. As the CPPN expresses within the central modules, **the neural network begins to redevelop based on the environment**, **the topology continuously expands**, and nerves gradually enter each module along the physical bus, establishing connections with the internal MCUs. The modules are awakened and integrated into the overarching network.
In the real-world environment, the phenotype interacts with the actual environment, generating corresponding **short-term memories** and **stress response habits**.
Alternatively, the **phenotypic parameters from the virtual environment can be directly flashed** into the MCUs of each module, allowing the physical individual to rapidly acquire the identical capabilities it had in the simulation, serving as a foundation for further adaptation and training.

## Training Method

Variable morphology and polymorphic expression of phenotypes cause **gradient shattering**, making it difficult to train the model via backpropagation and gradient descent. Furthermore, the multimodality of the scenarios makes it extremely difficult to design **reward functions** for traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL).

Drawing inspiration from the **RL** paradigm, we plan to use an external scoring mechanism based on a Critic network. In the virtual environment, fitness evaluations for multimodal scenario elements will be conducted by having the Critic observe the behavioral patterns of the individuals generated by the CPPN.

Through **Genetic Algorithms (GA)**, CPPN genes are eliminated based on the Critic's scores. An ideal genotype should encode a **body architecture** that can operate stably, as well as **initial instincts** geared toward the environment.

Evaluation and elimination should be conducted in stages, occurring after initial morphological stability is achieved, ensuring that individuals in the embryonic stage can develop normally without premature culling.

## Vision

When embodied artificial life—equipped with distributed neural networks, multimodal interaction capabilities, and synergistic hardware-software co-evolution—appears in the real world, it will no longer be static code running on a server, nor a rigid tool mapping fixed outputs to given inputs. It will possess instincts inherited from its genes and experience accumulated from its growth.

It will become a truly novel species characterized by **structural diversity**, **cognitive subjectivity**, and a **strong sense of physical presence**. This will fundamentally prove the foundational thesis of this research proposal: **Intelligence is not a pre-set endpoint; it is the fascinating byproduct inevitably evolved by life as it deeply integrates with its environment.**
